How will you rate housing options?

I'll be using three arbitrary categories: Location, Atmosphere, and Utility.

Each rating category will have a rank from D to S. S indicates that it's the best (or tied for best), and D indicates that it's the worst. The order from worst to best goes D-C-B-A-S.

When it comes to location, closer is better. Closer to classes, the gym, the dining hall, parking lots, just about anything. Since location importance is debatable (e.g. is being closer to classes more important than the dining hall?) it's important to determine which locations are considered more important and less important. These ratings are meant for the general student, so not an RA or an unusual student who doesn't ever go to class. Therefore, I will consider class location as the most important, with everything else supporting this category.

Atmosphere describes the environment that the dorm provides. This can be separated further into 1. physical atmosphere (meaning aesthetics, construction) and 2. social atmosphere (meaning proximity to other students, aptitude for social gatherings, proximity to parties, etc.)

Utility describes how useful the dorm is for enhancing your productivity and giving the resident options to thrive. This means space, convenience, etc., all within the dorm itself. This does not describe air conditioning but does describe things like its kitchen, the beds, how you navigate within the dorm, where its mailroom is, etc.

Alderman Hall-style Dorms

Also known as "new dorms", these are the most recently built (not renovated) dorms for first-years on grounds.

Residents live in doubles, meaning two residents per room, where there is a common bathroom for all the residents of a floor.

Advantages of new dorms:

  • Closest to O-Hill Dining Hall (best breakfast on grounds) and Slaughter Gym. Most of the dorms are also closest to the AFC, which is the best gym for most students on Grounds
  • Up to six stories high depending on the building, if you get a high floor you will probably have a nice view from your window
  • Most modern-looking from the outside
  • Study areas on each floor
  • Large common areas suitable for studying, first floors have very large common spaces and kitchen areas for cooking/eating
    •

    Disadvantages of new dorms:

  • Difference in interior quality varies heavily depending on which building
  • Hall-style bathrooms and resultant lack of in-room amenities

    I mentioned each building is different. There are a total of nine different buildings in new dorms, which all have their advantages and disadvantages.

    Balz-Dobie

    This dorm is almost exclusively for Echols and Rodman Scholars. Most of the students here are nerds, of the good and of the bad varieties. If someone says they live in Balz, you'll usually think "Yeah, that makes sense"

    I stayed in this dorm for my orientation, and it wasn't amazing. The lights didn't really work, the interior is bland, the bed is made out of plastic and also smelled like sweat for some reason.

    The beds are all pretty shitty on grounds to be honest, so we can get that out of the way now. Balz isn't especially shit or anything for that.

    Balz is cool because they do cool Echols/Rodman scholar things, I've seen workshops that Rodman scholars do where they have fun cooking activties and such. To be perfectly honest, the only particular disadvantage of Balz is the location. It's not a crazy hike, but it's not particularly close either. This hike is also shared with Watson-Webb and Kellogg.

    Ratings:

  • Location: B-
  • Atmosphere: B
  • Utility: A
